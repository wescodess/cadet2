Relay

A case moves like a relay baton between the client and the representative. Relay makes that hand-off the organising idea of the whole system while keeping Console’s look. Every client screen opens with a turn bar, “Your turn” beside “Priya’s turn”, so an anxious client never has to hunt. Every list row ends in a holder: who has this item now. The representative queue uses the same holder, so a representative sees at a glance which files are waiting on them and which are waiting on the client. Amber is always the client, blue is always the representative, in both portals.

  • One rule, both portals. Amber is the client, blue is the representative, green is done, violet is signed, wherever you are.
  • Every row has a holder. The right edge of a list says who has the item now, in a disc and a word.
  • Both turns at once. The turn bar shows what you owe and what Priya is doing, side by side, because both are usually true.
  • Console’s instrument underneath. Graphite, hairlines, Plex, filling circles and visible shortcuts are unchanged.

Specimen data: every name, licence number, case and date here is fictional. “cadet” is a working name. The content matches round 1 so the variations compare like for like.

Whose turn

Turn is not a ninth colour role. It maps each actor onto a role that already means the same thing: the client’s turn is caution (something is missing), the representative’s turn is progress (someone has it), done is positive and signed is seal. Relay uses the mapping everywhere, in both portals, so the colours mean the same thing to a client and to their representative.

Rules

  • Colour belongs to the actor, not the viewer: amber is the client even on the representative’s screen, where the label says “Client”.
  • A person is coloured only when it is their move. Presence avatars stay neutral.
  • Every disc has a word beside it. The holder is never a bare dot.
  • State chips keep their own colours. The holder says who; the chip says what.
  • Unassigned files are the practice’s turn, shown in blue with “Unassigned” and an empty person glyph.

Foundations

Console’s foundations, with the lake-blue progress role and one new idea carried through every component: the holder, a disc and a word that say who has an item now.

Typography
IBM Plex Sans for everything, with tabular figures. Plex Mono only for identifiers people copy: case IDs and licence numbers.
Colour logic
Graphite and white. Colour appears on states, links and focus, and on whose turn it is: amber for the client and blue for the representative, the same in both portals.
Density
Two settings of one scale. Representative controls are 30 px and a two-line queue row is about 80 px, with 14 px text; client controls are 48 px with 17 px text.
Shape
6 px corners in the representative portal, 10 to 14 px in the client portal. Hairline borders define every region.
Elevation
Flat surfaces and hairlines. Popovers, dialogs and toasts get one crisp shadow with a 1 px ring.
Iconography
Lucide at 1.75 px stroke. Work status uses custom filling circles; signed and delivered are solid discs.
Motion
Quick and quiet: 120 ms for hover and selection, 160 ms for panels. No bounce, no stagger.
Focus
A 2 px blue outline, the only blue in the interface, so focus is never mistaken for state.
