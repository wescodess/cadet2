4. Case Record

A Case is one continuous, numbered record: people and sharing, safety and scope, facts, questions, dates, submissions, the handoff, Draft findings, proposed text and the review. A section index keeps your place. A margin beside the text carries who supplied or checked each thing, sources and private notes. You edit a section in place. The caseload is a sortable table.

  • Read the whole Case top to bottom, in the order an auditor or reviewer would.
  • The margin answers “who says so?” beside every section: Client, Staff, cadet2 or a source.
  • Private notes live only in the margin; proposed Client text sits in the record, labelled.
  • One section is edited at a time, with its own save state and submit.

Synthetic pilot. Every legal sentence is the registry’s draft text, marked “Draft wording, pending counsel”, with its registry key. Names, dates, contacts and identifiers are fictional fixtures. The desk is shown at 1280 px; a dashed line marks the bottom of a 1280 by 800 window.

Best at
Defensibility and reading: the full Case, origins and sources in one place, in a stable order that matches a future Signed plan and export; linear at 200% zoom.
Worst at
Speed and turn-taking: long pages, the current task is one section among nine, and the turn line at the top scrolls away; editing in place inside a long document makes focus and conflicts harder.
What to watch
Whether Staff find the one section they need to act on, and whether the margin is read or ignored.
