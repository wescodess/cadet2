3. Stations

Each Case moves through six named stations: connection and sharing, facts, check and submit, safety and scope, Draft findings, then notes and the review request. A rail shows every station’s state and holder. Each station is one page: the work on the left, and on the right what’s still open there, who holds it and the gate to the next station.

  • Gates are visible: you see why the next station is closed and who can open it.
  • Each station has one primary action, and submitting or requesting review is always its own step.
  • The caseload is a board of the same stations, each card ending in its holder.
  • Stations describe work, never progress toward approval.

Synthetic pilot. Every legal sentence is the registry’s draft text, marked “Draft wording, pending counsel”, with its registry key. Names, dates, contacts and identifiers are fictional fixtures. The desk is shown at 1280 px; a dashed line marks the bottom of a 1280 by 800 window.

Best at
Preventing the wrong step: accidental submission, Staff acknowledging or a review request that hides open items; each blocker names who holds it; the clearest training path for new Staff.
Worst at
Real work loops back: a clarification sends a Case back to Facts while a Draft already exists, and the six-column board is cramped at 1280 px; it can be read as a progress bar toward approval.
What to watch
Whether practitioners accept the fixed order, and whether a Case sitting at two stations at once (a Client question open while a Draft is ready) is understood.
